Abstract

Active noise cancellation (ANC) headphones can almost silence the drone of an aircraft engine and yet the slam of a door is still audible. This article explains why. ANC works by measuring incoming sound with microphones and playing an inverted copy of it, so the two waves cancel by destructive interference. Sudden sounds are hard for three reasons: they are unpredictable, they are mostly high frequency, and they are too brief for the adaptive filter to adjust. Table 1 demonstrates an example of a delay; a lag of just 0.1 ms means a 2 kHz sound ends up louder instead of quieter. Noise cancellation and robust algorithms minimise the effect of the sudden sounds heard by the headphones, however due to the physics of the delay, not all the noise can be fully diminished.
